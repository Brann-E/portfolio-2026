Metaliminality. Seeing together, and being able to act.

There is a moment when a collective sees, together, what circulates through its bonds - and begins, for that very reason, to be able to act on it. This moment has no name in design. Here is one, and the exact conditions of its effectiveness.

9 min read

The previous text established two things. That circulation, invisible and situated, requires a threshold where views are composed in order to be seen. And that a cooperation, unlike the initiate who crosses alone, must cross this threshold collectively.

This threshold exists. I propose to name it metaliminality: the position from which one sees the complete geography of a circulation before acting on it - not from a point in the system, but from a plane above the positions, where they all become visible at once. The prefix meta doesn't say “beyond” in the vague sense; it says exactly this: one notch above the ordinary liminal threshold, the place from which the thresholds are seen.

But naming is not enough, and it would even be the surest way to miss the point. Because metaliminality, poorly understood, is only contemplation - a lovely moment of shared lucidity that changes nothing. This whole text rests on a single gap: the one that separates seeing from being able to.

The trap: seeing is not being able to

We must face the most serious objection head-on, because it targets the heart of relational work. You can understand a system perfectly and remain unable to change anything in it.

An employee can have an absolutely fine reading of their organization - see every asymmetry, every poorly distributed flow of recognition - and remain held by their contract, their dependence, their fear. A collective can map beautifully what circulates between its members and draw no action from it. Lucidity alone produces lucid and powerless people. It can even make things worse: clearly seeing an injustice you cannot correct is a suffering, not a liberation.

A method that stops at diagnosis produces nothing. The real threshold is not between ignorance and understanding: it is between understanding and the act.

This is where the concept must be built with rigor, otherwise it collapses. What turns a shared vision into a capacity to act? The answer is not “more vision”. It is a change in the status of what is seen.

What changes everything: knowledge becomes common

Let us take the lucid employee again. What paralyzes them is not that they are unaware of something - it is that they are alone in knowing it, or that they don't know whether others know it too. Acting alone on an asymmetry that only you perceive is costly and risky: you expose yourself, you can be disowned, you can end up making the accusation in the open. The same man, in a room where everyone has just seen the same asymmetry and knows that the others have seen it, is no longer in the same situation at all - even though he has learned nothing new about the system itself.

This distinction has a precise name, formalized by game theory: common knowledge. It is not enough for everyone to know. Everyone must know that the others know, and know that they know that he knows - in a cascade. It is a much stronger property than the mere fact that information is distributed.

The economist Michael Chwe demonstrated it in the most striking way. Why do great public ceremonies have such power? Not because they transmit content from a source to each spectator - but because they allow everyone to see that the others see. They manufacture common knowledge. And Chwe shows that this is exactly what coordination needs: rising up, coordinating, acting together are problems that common knowledge solves and that merely distributed knowledge leaves unsolvable. It is no accident, he notes, that authoritarian regimes first censor gatherings and public communications: they are not afraid that people know, they are afraid that people know that others know.

Metaliminality doesn't manufacture lucidity. It manufactures common knowledge, and it is common knowledge that makes action possible.

This is what definitively moves the concept out of contemplation. Metaliminality is not an inner state of the designer who would see better than others. It is a collective operation: the moment when a group goes from a sum of private perceptions to a common perception, and where each person, knowing that the others see what they see, stops being alone facing what should be done.

Contemplating is not refraining from acting

One resistance remains, a more philosophical one. Our era holds contemplation to be the opposite of action: looking would be doing nothing. By that measure, metaliminality - this time when we stop to see - would pass for a luxury, a pause before the serious things.

The philosopher Byung-Chul Han overturned this self-evidence. In The Burnout Society, then in Vita contemplativa, he argues that contemporary exhaustion comes precisely from the erasure of the vita contemplativa in favor of a vita activa that has become hyperactive and empty. We act constantly and transform nothing, because we have lost the ability to stop in order to see. For Han, contemplation is not the opposite of action: it is its condition. Action that is not preceded by a time of vision is mere agitation.

This is exactly the status of metaliminality. It is not a pause before action, it is that without which action is mere agitation. The collective that rushes into solutions without having seen together what circulates stirs up activity and moves nothing. The slowness of seeing is the condition of the rightness of doing.

The concept
Metaliminality
The operation by which situated, fragmentary perceptions of the same circulation become common knowledge, to the point where each person sees their place in a whole of which they know the others see it too.
It is not part of the transformation: it makes it possible. It is not a posture of the designer, but a moment that is organized, prepared, and reopened. And it is not contemplative in the passive sense: it is the condition of right action.

A regime, not a stage

One might object: isn't this simply diagnosis, framing, preparation? The answer is clear. A preparation takes place once, then we move on. Metaliminality, for its part, is reopened. In the arrangements that hold, the collective periodically redoes the exercise of seeing itself from the whole - a regular meeting, a ritual, a shared reading of what circulates. It is therefore not the first step of a linear sequence: it is a level of perception that is reinstalled at intervals, and that can return at any moment of the work. We don't “do” metaliminality at the start to forget it afterward; we build the conditions for the collective to be able to reopen it without us.

The four conditions of passage

Common knowledge is necessary. It is not sufficient. For a shared vision to truly become a capacity to act, four conditions must be met - and experience shows that they easily fall, each defining a precise failure mode.

01
Mutual visibility
Perceptions must become visible to everyone in such a way that each person can see that they are visible to the others. This can happen in the same room, in a mediated presence or through a durably accessible shared trace. Without this mutual visibility, knowledge remains distributed.
02
The grip
The representation must show each person their lever, not just the whole. A map that shows the entire system without showing where you can act from your place produces exactly the powerless lucidity described above.
03
Inscription
What has been seen together must settle into something durable - a convention, a rule, a regular arrangement. Common knowledge that is inscribed nowhere dissipates between two meetings, and all that remains is the vague memory of a good moment. That is why relational work produces objects, not reports.
04
Safety
Acting must be possible without disproportionate risk. This is not a moral precaution but an operating condition: each person intervenes only where they feel legitimate and safe. An arrangement that requires people to expose themselves beyond what they can bear gets only a polite silence; and knowledge, unable to be said, ceases to be shared.

And sometimes, there is no grip

It must be said, because it is the counterpart of the honesty demanded above. It happens that pooling reveals that there is no room for action where one hoped. That the interests at play are truly incompatible, not just misunderstood. That the blockage comes from a material constraint, a balance of power, a limit that no relational arrangement will move.

Discovering this is a result, not a failure - and often a valuable result. It spares months of arrangements that would have changed nothing, and it opens other paths: negotiating, compensating, limiting a power, separating, or owning a conflict rather than disguising it as a communication problem. An honest metaliminality must be able to conclude “there is nothing to do here”. It is even by this possibility that we recognize it is an inquiry, and not a sale.

Lineages
What this concept owes to others
The moment that metaliminality names has neighbors that it would be dishonest to leave unmentioned. As early as 1970, John Chris Jones spoke of metadesign to designate the design of design processes. Gerhard Fischer and Elisa Giaccardi made it a framework aimed at creating the infrastructures where others can in turn design. Donald Schön had described problem setting, the framing work that precedes any resolution. What metaliminality adds is the shift of this moment from the designer to the collective - and its articulation with common knowledge, which makes it not a state of lucidity but the precise threshold where seeing becomes being able to.

One question remains that this text left open: what, concretely, makes things circulate between people? Metaliminality, by enabling the setting up of common knowledge, explains how a circulation becomes visible and actionable, once it takes place. It doesn't say where the impulse comes from that pushes someone to give, to contribute, to commit to another. For that, we must go down to the foundation - to the oldest mechanism of circulation that human societies have invented. That is the subject of the next text.

References

The sources drawn on in this text, and the precise point on which each is called upon.

Common knowledge & coordination

  • Michael Suk-Young Chwe, Rational Ritual: Culture, Coordination, and Common Knowledge, Princeton, Princeton University Press, 2001. Heart of the text: common knowledge as a condition of coordination, the function of public rituals and ceremonies, and the targeted censorship of public communications by authoritarian regimes.
  • David K. Lewis, Convention: A Philosophical Study, Cambridge, Harvard University Press, 1969. Philosophical origin of the notion of common knowledge, on which the whole later tradition in game theory and social science rests.

Contemplation & action

  • Byung-Chul Han, The Burnout Society (Müdigkeitsgesellschaft, 2010), Stanford, Stanford University Press, 2015. Drawn on for contemporary exhaustion as the erasure of the vita contemplativa in favor of an empty hyperactivity.
  • Byung-Chul Han, Vita contemplativa. Éloge de l'inactivité (2022), Paris, Actes Sud, 2024 (French edition). Contemplation set not as the opposite of action but as its condition - direct support for the thesis “metaliminality is not a pause before action, it makes it right”.

Metadesign & framing

  • John Chris Jones, Design Methods: Seeds of Human Futures, London, John Wiley & Sons, 1970. Origin of the notion of metadesign - the design of design and change processes.
  • Elisa Giaccardi & Gerhard Fischer, “Creativity and Evolution: A Metadesign Perspective”, Digital Creativity, vol. 19, no. 1, 2008. Metadesign as the design of the social and technical infrastructures where others can in turn design.
  • Donald A. Schön, The Reflective Practitioner: How Professionals Think in Action, New York, Basic Books, 1983. Problem setting - the framing of the problem that precedes and conditions its resolution.
