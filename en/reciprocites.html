What wears down a relationship is rarely what we look at.

This page presents a method for cooperation between people and between organizations. It extends, beyond the product, the reading I apply in my projects: looking at what circulates between people, and what has stopped circulating.

People have been taught to coordinate, not to meet. To produce arrangements, not to design relationships. To measure results, not to take care of what makes them possible.

This is not inevitable. It is a mechanism, and a mechanism can be repaired.

A broken link between the gesture and its value

Since the industrial revolution, work systems have cut the human into functional units. We no longer ask who you are, we ask what you produce. Your value is measurable, your humanity is not, so it disappears from the system.

We have built extraordinary systems for working together. Tools, methods, meetings, charters, contracts, impact indicators, and so many others. And yet. Trust dissipates. Partnerships wear out. Work piles up without being recognized. We coordinate but no longer meet. We deliver but no longer understand each other.

And yet, everyone knows this is not how they want to work.

And the loop closes: the exhausted ecosystem reinforces the paradigm, which shrinks people even more, who damage collectives even more. A downward spiral that feeds on itself.

The vertigo comes from their number, not from their difficulty.

They intertwine, and each seems to call for its own answer. It is this impression of entanglement that discourages, we don't know where to start, so we don't start. Yet each of these places calls for a gesture of care. And none is a procedure: these are gestures that anyone can hold starting tomorrow.

A praxis is a way of envisioning the world and acting on it. Not a method one applies: a way of being one holds. It is injected into ordinary gestures: the way of opening a meeting, naming a disagreement, recognizing what someone brings. Nothing changes in the program; everything changes in what happens in it.

A gesture leaves no trace. We need spaces to make it exist and endure.

A gesture holds in the moment. But as soon as a situation involves several people, spreads over time or exceeds what a conversation can carry, something else is needed: a way to keep a trace, make visible, share, discuss. This is what the method brings, not steps to follow, but a soil where praxis can take root.

The method today produces one object: the reciprocity agreement. It is neither the purpose nor the mandatory step, it is the first medium we knew how to build so that a reciprocity leaves a trace.

Roles

The Binder

Guardian of the process. They carry no project and decide nothing in place of others: they create the conditions so that what already exists can be seen, said and held.

The Recipreneur

The one who takes the initiative. They seize a reciprocity, gather the people concerned, carry the initial intention without being the guarantor of the process.

The Reciprocant

The one who enters the reciprocity. They bring what they have, receive what circulates, and commit with others.

These roles are not statuses. A reciprocant can become a recipreneur elsewhere; a recipreneur can learn to bind. It is a path, not a hierarchy.

Before committing: sizing up

This whole stage is carried out by two: the recipreneur, who carries the intention, and the binder, who holds the process. The reciprocants only come in afterward. Praxis starts here, not by promising a transformation, but by honestly looking at what is feasible, and at what height. Three questions: what we want to accomplish, what it weighs, what we can carry. As long as we haven't answered them, we don't commit.

Questioning sizing along two axes

Two questions are enough to place a project: what means do we have, and what degree of complexity are we dealing with. The crossing of the two doesn't say whether a project is good: it says at what height it can hold today.

Capacity - what comes from us
  • The time we can truly devote to it
  • The legitimacy recognized in the project lead
  • The key skills already there
  • The ability to steer and to decide
  • The resources we can mobilize
  • Resilience in the face of the unexpected
  • Continuity beyond individuals
×
Complexity - what comes from the situation
  • The number of actors to bring on board
  • The distance between their worlds
  • The gap between their interests
  • The entanglement of exchanges
  • The weight of legal or political stakes
  • The extent of the time horizon
  • The difficulty of backtracking
What reading the two axes reveals, to commit at the right height
Simply exist

When complexity is low and we have more than enough to carry it. Nothing to weigh down: we move as we see, harvest quickly, keep the gesture light.

Phase it

When complexity rises but capacity roughly keeps up. We don't commit everything at once: we cut into milestones, test, adjust at each loop before going further.

Structure

When complexity is real and we have the means to meet it. The time to set the frame: clear roles, named responsibilities, reliable timelines and landmarks so that it holds.

Architect

When complexity is high and capacity solid. We no longer steer a project but a system: multiple agreements, distributed commitments, explicit steering that coordinates the whole.

Reframe

When complexity exceeds what we can carry today. It is not a failure: it is the signal that we must reduce the scope, postpone, or strengthen capacity before restarting.

None of these positions is better than another. They only say at what height a project can hold today, and a project changes position as capacity is built.

Where I step in.

I step in as a binder. I don't carry the projects and I decide nothing in place of anyone, I create the conditions for actors to manage to cooperate, and to hold over time. But I don't settle for a remote diagnosis or a workshop that is forgotten after a few days. I design, I build, I produce the objects to construct reciprocities. Where others would put in place a contract - a bond that obliges, with its debt and its sanction -, I build an agreement: an accord that we choose for ourselves and uphold, because each person finds a part of themselves in it.

Kindness can't be built. Reciprocity can.

That is the difference between a posture and a mechanism. Between an intention and an infrastructure. Many approaches today speak of relationship and cooperation - but most treat the relationship as a value, something we encourage and celebrate. I treat it as a material: something we design, name, share, pass on.

And the difference shows afterward. When enthusiasm fades. When the first people leave. When the break comes.
